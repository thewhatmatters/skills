# Startup research

One job: **reduce the cost of being wrong**. Every step below serves that. Research with no decision attached is a hobby — stop and ask what decision this feeds.

## 1. Frame before you gather

Spend disproportionate time on *what* before *how*:

1. Turn a vague ask (“should we build this?”) into a **sharp, falsifiable question** (e.g. “will SMB users pay $X/mo to solve Y, and is that market big enough to matter?”).
2. Name the **decision** this research feeds and who owns it.
3. **Size effort to stakes** — reversible ~$5k call → an afternoon; strategic bet → days/weeks. Timebox to “good enough to decide,” not “publishable.”
4. Write the question, decision, stakes, and timebox in one short block before any search or interview.

If the ask cannot be made falsifiable, ask the decision-maker one clarifying question — then proceed.

## 2. Gather across modes (range over single-method depth)

Move between modes; do not over-index on one:

| Mode | Use when | Examples |
|------|----------|----------|
| Qualitative | Need motives, language, willingness | Interviews, expert calls, ethnography; non-leading questions |
| Quantitative | Need magnitude, cohorts, funnels | Surveys, analytics, basic stats (enough not to fool yourself with a tiny *n* or a p-value) |
| Desk | Need market/competitive/public signal | TAM/SAM/SOM, teardowns, filings, patents, papers, public data |
| Experiments | Need faster learning than study | A/B, fake-door, landing-page smoke tests, prototypes |

Be resourceful: competitor job posts, Reddit, app-store reviews, support tickets count as data. Prefer the existing [deep-research](sand-workflow:deep-research) skill for multi-pass desk sweeps when the question is landscape/competitive/market and needs a cited report.

## 3. Reason without self-deception

- Separate **correlation from causation**; signal from noise in small messy sets.
- Actively hunt **disconfirming** evidence; do not collect only support for a pre-baked conclusion.
- **Name assumptions** and state confidence honestly (e.g. “high on the problem, low on willingness to pay”).
- **Triangulate** — trust more when interviews, data, and market behavior agree.

## 4. Deliver something people act on

Lead with the **answer and recommendation**, not the methodology.

1. One-line call for a founder; nuance for a PM — match the audience.
2. Quantify impact where you can; be explicit about **limitations** and **what would change your mind**.
3. Stop at diminishing returns. If confidence is still too low for the stakes, say what one next probe would raise it most.

## 5. Meta bar

- Domain fluency enough to ask smart questions (tech, business model, market).
- Tool fluency as available (SQL, surveys, analytics; AI for synthesis/literature acceleration — never as a substitute for primary evidence).
- Intellectual honesty under pressure — tell the founder the thing they do not want to hear when the evidence says so.

## Anti-patterns

- Methodology theater with no decision.
- Single-source conviction.
- Confidence laundering (pretty prose over thin evidence).
- Endless research past the timebox.
- Shipping a report that does not change what someone will do Monday.
